About Pope Francis

Pope Francis was the head of the Catholic Church and sovereign of the Vatican City State from 2013 until his death in 2025. He was the first Jesuit pope, the first from the Americas and from the Southern Hemisphere, and the first born outside of Europe since the 8th-century Syrian pope Gregory III.

Throughout his public life, Francis was noted for his humility, emphasis on God's mercy, international visibility as pope, concern for the poor, and commitment to interreligious dialogue.

Early Life

Born Jorge Mario Bergoglio on 17 December 1936 in Flores, Buenos Aires, he was the eldest of five children of Mario José Bergoglio and Regina María Sívori. His father was an Italian immigrant accountant from Piedmont, and his mother was a housewife born in Buenos Aires to a family of northern Italian origin.

In his youth, Bergoglio worked as a chemical technician before beginning seminary studies. He recovered from a severe illness in his youth, which resulted in the removal of part of one of his lungs.

Jesuit Years

Bergoglio entered the Society of Jesus in 1958 and was ordained to the priesthood in 1969. He served as the Jesuit provincial superior in Argentina from 1973 to 1979.

"As a Jesuit, Bergoglio was known for his humility, doctrinal conservatism, and commitment to social justice."

Papacy (2013-2025)

Key Achievements

  • Made action on climate change a leading focus of his papacy
  • Committed to worldwide abolition of the death penalty
  • Promoted interreligious dialogue and peace initiatives
  • Reformed Vatican finances and bureaucracy

Legacy

Francis was known for his less formal approach to the papacy, choosing to reside in the Domus Sanctae Marthae guesthouse rather than the papal apartments. He emphasized mercy, humility, and service to the poor throughout his papacy.
